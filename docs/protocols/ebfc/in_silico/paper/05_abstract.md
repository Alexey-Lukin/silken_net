We ask how far a computed driving force can be read against experiment for the FAD-dehydrogenase→osmium step of an enzymatic biofuel cell, with one affordable B3LYP protocol on component models: lumiflavin, an osmium(III/II) complex and a Cu–Co node of the Co–Cu–Ce framework cathode. The two-electron, two-proton flavin potential lies 65 mV above experiment at a screening level without the flavin's zero-point energy and ≈210 mV below it with that term; no level validates it. A Hammett trend for the mediator (−0.92 eV per σ unit per complex) agrees in direction with three measured series, with mixed donor-side magnitudes and a 1.2- to 3-fold acceptor-side overshoot. Like for like on one SHE scale, the cascade is downhill in all 32 B3LYP/6-31G(d) cells (smallest +218.5 mV; measured +529 mV) but uphill for the device couple on the relaxed electronic flavin level with both sides re-read at ωB97X/def2-TZVP (−137 mV), and spin–orbit coupling on osmium, omitted here, reverses 2 of them at the −250 mV the literature computes for [Os(bpy)₃], more at the larger shifts it computes for other ligand sets, and against the measured flavin the device couple keeps +70 mV at −250 mV and turns uphill past −320 mV, so the protocol does not determine the sign. Its gap to experiment moves by hundreds of millivolts with protocol choices; on our reference convention the closest agreements are cancellations. Cathode direct electron transfer lies below turnover at the computed Co reorganisation energy and straddles it at an assumed one; its charge-transfer resistance is bracketed, not predicted.
